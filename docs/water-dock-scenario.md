# Scenario Template — Dock Construction by the Water

## Scenario Title

**Waterfront Dock Construction**

## Scenario Description / Instructions

You are reviewing an active dock construction site beside a body of water. The work area includes a partially built wooden or concrete dock, workers near the water’s edge, construction materials, temporary power, lifting equipment, and public or vehicle access nearby.

Identify the major hazards in the scene and explain how they should be controlled before work continues. For each hazard, describe what is unsafe, where it is located, and what control or mitigation should be put in place.

---

# Correct Hazards and Controls

## 1. Unprotected Water Edge / Fall Into Water

**Hazard Description:**  
Workers are operating near the edge of the dock or shoreline without adequate fall protection, guardrails, or water-rescue controls. A worker could slip, trip, or lose balance and fall into the water, especially while carrying tools or materials.

**Correct Control or Mitigation:**  
Install temporary guardrails where practical, require personal flotation devices near the water, provide rescue equipment such as life rings and throw ropes, and establish a water rescue plan. Keep walking surfaces clear and use spotters when workers are close to the edge.

**Location Note on Image:**  
Along the open dock edge or shoreline where workers are close to the water.

---

## 2. Missing or Inadequate Life-Saving Equipment

**Hazard Description:**  
The work area is near deep or moving water, but life-saving equipment is not visible, is too far away, or is blocked by materials. If someone falls into the water, response time could be delayed.

**Correct Control or Mitigation:**  
Place life rings, throw bags, rescue poles, and emergency ladders in visible, accessible locations. Workers should know where the rescue equipment is and how to use it. Emergency access routes should remain clear.

**Location Note on Image:**  
Near the dock entrance, along the water edge, or beside a blocked rescue station.

---

## 3. Unstable Materials Stored Near Dock Edge

**Hazard Description:**  
Lumber, pipes, concrete forms, or metal beams are stacked close to the edge of the dock. Materials could roll, slide, fall into the water, or strike workers nearby.

**Correct Control or Mitigation:**  
Store materials in a designated laydown area away from edges. Use chocks, straps, racks, or blocking to prevent movement. Keep walkways and emergency access routes clear.

**Location Note on Image:**  
Near the dock edge or beside the partially built structure.

---

## 4. Wet / Slippery Walking Surface

**Hazard Description:**  
The dock surface is wet from rain, splashing water, mud, or spilled materials. This creates a slip hazard, especially for workers carrying tools, walking near the edge, or stepping onto temporary planks.

**Correct Control or Mitigation:**  
Use slip-resistant walking surfaces, clean mud or spills promptly, improve drainage, and mark slippery areas. Workers should wear proper slip-resistant safety footwear.

**Location Note on Image:**  
On the dock deck, temporary walkway, or transition area between the shore and dock.

---

## 5. Temporary Electrical Power Near Water

**Hazard Description:**  
Extension cords, generators, or temporary lighting are being used close to water or wet surfaces. Damaged cables or improper grounding could expose workers to electric shock.

**Correct Control or Mitigation:**  
Use GFCI-protected circuits, elevate or cover cables, inspect cords for damage, and keep electrical equipment away from standing water. Use weather-rated equipment suitable for outdoor wet locations.

**Location Note on Image:**  
Near power tools, a generator, temporary lights, or cables crossing the dock.

---

## 6. Equipment Operating Too Close to the Edge

**Hazard Description:**  
A forklift, skid steer, crane, or small excavator is operating near the dock edge or unstable ground. The equipment could tip, collapse the edge, or strike workers.

**Correct Control or Mitigation:**  
Establish equipment exclusion zones, verify ground stability and load limits, keep equipment a safe distance from edges, and use a trained spotter. Heavy equipment should only travel on approved routes.

**Location Note on Image:**  
On the shore side near the dock entrance or beside the partially built dock edge.

---

## 7. Inadequate Barricade Between Public Area and Work Zone

**Hazard Description:**  
The dock construction area is not fully separated from pedestrians, boaters, or nearby vehicle traffic. Unauthorized people could enter the work zone and be exposed to water hazards, equipment, tools, or unstable surfaces.

**Correct Control or Mitigation:**  
Install fencing, rigid barricades, warning signs, and controlled entry points. Maintain a clear separation between the public and construction activities. Use spotters or flaggers where vehicles or deliveries cross public access areas.

**Location Note on Image:**  
At the dock entrance, shoreline path, marina walkway, or parking/loading area.
