# Sample Responses — Shopping Mall Atrium Renovation

## Scenario

**Scenario Title:** Shopping Mall Atrium Renovation

The user is reviewing an active renovation area inside a shopping mall atrium. The scene includes public walkways near the work zone, overhead work, a scissor lift near an escalator opening, exposed temporary electrical cables, and poorly stored materials near a storefront opening.

The goal of each response is to identify the hazards, explain why they are unsafe, and describe appropriate controls or mitigations.

---

# 1. Perfect Response — High Proficiency

In this shopping mall atrium renovation scene, I can see several hazards that need to be controlled before work continues.

First, there is an inadequate public barrier between the active construction area and the nearby mall walkway. The barrier looks incomplete, and there appears to be enough space for a shopper or unauthorized person to enter the work zone. This is unsafe because members of the public could be exposed to construction debris, tools, moving equipment, or overhead work. The area should be fully separated using solid hoarding, rigid barricades, clear warning signage, and controlled access points. If construction traffic has to cross a public walkway, a spotter or temporary pedestrian control should be used.

Second, there is overhead work above a walkway. Workers or materials are positioned at an upper level near the atrium ceiling or balcony area, and people may be passing underneath. This creates a falling-object hazard if tools, fixtures, or materials are dropped. The area below should be treated as an exclusion zone. Workers should use tool lanyards, toe boards, debris netting, and proper elevated work platforms. No workers or pedestrians should be allowed below active overhead work.

Third, the scissor lift is positioned too close to an escalator opening or atrium edge. This creates a serious risk of tipping, striking the escalator structure, or exposing the worker to a fall or crush hazard. The lift should be moved to a level, stable surface with proper clearance from edges, escalators, and openings. The work area around the lift should be barricaded, and the lift should be inspected before use. The operator should follow the manufacturer's operating limits and keep the platform guardrails in place.

Fourth, there are exposed electrical cables running across the floor. These temporary cords create a trip hazard, especially on a polished mall floor where workers, carts, or pedestrians may pass through. The cables could also become damaged and create an electrical shock risk. Cables should be routed overhead where possible or protected using cord ramps or cable covers. They should be inspected for damage, kept out of walkways, and connected through GFCI protection.

Fifth, there is poor material storage near a storefront opening. Materials such as glass panels, drywall sheets, metal studs, or tiles appear to be stacked loosely and may be leaning or blocking access. This creates struck-by, trip, and blocked-egress hazards. Materials should be stored in a designated laydown area using racks, straps, or chocks where needed. Storefront openings, emergency paths, and mall corridors should be kept clear.

Overall, the main controls are to separate the public from the construction zone, protect people from overhead work, keep mobile equipment away from edges, manage temporary electrical cords safely, and store materials in a controlled area. The work should not continue until these hazards are corrected.

# 2. Okay Response — Moderate Proficiency

There are a few unsafe things in this mall construction area.

One issue is that the public barrier is not good enough. The construction area is too close to where people could walk, so someone could accidentally enter the work zone. They should put up better fencing or hoarding and signs so shoppers do not come into the area.

There is also work happening above the walkway. This is dangerous because tools or materials could fall on people below. The area underneath should be blocked off, and workers should secure their tools and materials.

The scissor lift also looks too close to the escalator or edge. It could be unsafe if it moves or tips. It should be moved farther away from the opening and used on a stable surface. The area around it should be blocked off.

There are cables on the floor, which could make someone trip. They should be covered with cable ramps or moved away from the walkway. The cords should also be checked so they are not damaged.

There are materials stacked near a storefront. They look like they could fall or block the way. They should be stored better in a proper storage area.

Overall, the scene needs better barriers, better housekeeping, safer equipment placement, and better control of people walking near the construction area.

# 3. Terrible Response — Low Proficiency

The mall construction site looks mostly fine. I do not see anything too serious.

There are some barriers, so people probably know not to go there. The workers just need to be careful. If someone walks into the area, they should just watch where they are going.

The people working above should try not to drop anything. I do not think it needs to be blocked off unless something actually falls.

The scissor lift is near the escalator, but that is probably because they need it there. As long as the person driving it is experienced, it should be okay.

There are some wires on the ground, but people can step over them. It is not a big problem. Maybe just tell everyone to pay attention.

The materials near the storefront are also probably there because they are being used soon. I would leave them there unless they are in the way.
